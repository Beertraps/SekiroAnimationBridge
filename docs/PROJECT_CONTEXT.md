## The idea

Biped needs no bone map between two Bipeds because every Biped is built from the same template and its motion is stored per template part. Full-body retargeting systems such as HumanIK work the same way for other skeletons: each skeleton is characterized once, which assigns its bones to the template's parts, the motion moves from template to template, and a full-body IK pass restores the contacts that other proportions break. The full-body bake does both steps itself. The characterization comes from the matcher's reading of names and anatomy, so in most cases nobody maps anything.

## Method

1. Characterization, `mapping.characterize(bones)`. The matcher's `Rig` finds the hips, spine, neck, head, clavicles, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, calves, feet, toes and fingers, by name where it is sure and by the skeleton's shape otherwise. One check was added: a clavicle found by shape must sit nearer the middle of the body than the hip joint on its side. Without it, an upper arm with a twist bone in series in a rig without clavicles read as clavicle and upper arm. The result lists which parts came from the shape, and the bake names them in its notes.
2. Pairing by the template, `mapping.body_pairs(source, target)`. The hips, head, hands, feet, toes, metacarpals and the last bone of each finger pair directly. Chains run between joints that both rigs have: the spine from its first bone through the chest to the neck, the neck to the head, an arm through its shoulder (and clavicle, where both rigs have one) and elbow to the wrist, a leg through its knee to the ankle, a finger from its first bone to its last. Between two such joints, bones pair one to one where both rigs have the same number. Otherwise a target bone takes the source's motion at the same share of the way along the chain, by length: the lower bone of a two-bone spine spans the first two bones of a three-bone spine, and a twist bone 40% down a forearm takes 40% of the way from the forearm's turn to the hand's, which spreads the wrist's twist. Target bones without a counterpart, such as clavicles the source does not have, do what **Unmapped bones** says.
3. Chain transfer, `fullbody.turns`. Each chain bone takes the two source bones' turns at its spot, blended by its share, which carries the twist; then it swings so that its segment lies along the source chain over the same share of the way. Bones outside the chains (hips, head, hands, feet, toes, last finger bones) take their source bone's turn, as in the bone-by-bone bake.
4. Contacts, `fullbody.keep_contacts`, after the chain transfer and before the weapons go into their grips.
   - Feet. Each foot's goal is the source's foot measured from the source's hips, scaled by the bake's travel scale (the ratio of hip heights above the feet) and measured from the target's hips. A source foot that stands still gives a goal that stands still, so a planted foot does not slide. The goal then moves sideways to the target's own hip width, as described below. The pelvis first drops by the least amount that lets both legs reach 99.99% of their length, at most a quarter of the shorter leg, eased over 0.1 s. Each leg then reaches by two-bone IK: the knee keeps the side it bends to, or bends toward the body's front where the leg is straight, and the foot keeps its rotation.
   - Hands. Where the source's wrists come within 0.6 of its forearm's length of each other, the target's wrists move to the source's distance and direction apart, scaled by arm length. Each wrist moves half the way; where only one hand holds a weapon, the free hand moves all the way, so the weapon stays where the bake put it. The weight fades out by one forearm length and is averaged over 0.05 s.
   - Hips and head keep the source's rotations. Elbows and knees keep pointing where the chain transfer turned them, which is where the source's point: the IK keeps each limb's bend side.
5. Reused unchanged: travel scale and the bone that carries body travel, root motion, weapons in the hand, weapon grips, Second hand (it still puts the free hand on the grip), body clearance, arm distance and the FromSoftware foot target chains, which stand on the final feet. `retarget.move_hand` gained an optional pole. Bones outside the body, such as weapons and helpers, take their source bones from the bone list, or from automatic matching where the list is empty.

## Solver: closed form, not iTaSC

Blender's iTaSC solver was checked in background Blender 4.5.14 with scratch scripts outside the repository. Pole targets work with iTaSC as with the standard solver, and no frame depended on earlier frames in the modes tried. iTaSC was not chosen because:

- The bake computes its own armature-space pose matrices per frame and refuses targets with constraints. iTaSC would need IK constraints on the target, a depsgraph evaluation per frame to read the result back, and their removal afterwards.
- Every limb is a two-joint chain. Two-bone IK is exact in closed form, and `move_hand` already solves it for Second hand and the fixes.
- The one coupling between goals, the pelvis drop shared by both legs, also has a closed-form answer: the least drop with which both legs reach.
- Closed form takes no iterations or tolerances, does not depend on the order of frames, and gives the same result every time, which the tests check to 0.01 mm.

What iTaSC would add is many weighted goals solved at once, such as the spine bending to help a hand reach. None of the contacts here needs that.

## The target's own hip width

Scaling the source's footprint by hip height keeps planted feet planted, but on its own it also carries over the source's stance width. Wolf's hip joints sit 8.6 cm closer together than the Owl's, scaled, and 4.2 cm farther apart than Isshin's, so his feet stood that much too wide on the Owl's clip and too narrow on Isshin's. Bone by bone does not do this, because each leg leans like the source's from the target's own hip joint.

- Each foot keeps the source's sideways distance from its own hip joint, scaled. The goal moves by how much farther out the target's hip joint sits than the source's, scaled, measured from the hips along the line through both hip joints at rest. The feet then stand the target's own hip width apart plus the source's spread beyond its hip width, scaled, and each leg leans out as far as the source's. Forward and back, the footprint stays the source's, scaled.
- The move points along the pelvis's sideways direction, kept level. While the foot stands, the move holds still at the mean of that direction over the stand. Otherwise a pelvis that turns over a planted foot would swing the move around the foot and slide it, by up to the move's size: 2.1 cm on Isshin→Wolf, 4.3 cm on Owl→Wolf.
- While the foot steps, before its first stand and after its last, the move turns with the pelvis. It stays off the pelvis's direction by an angle that eases from the one at the end of the stand before to the one at the start of the stand after, so it joins each stand without a jump. A first version turned the move straight from one stand's direction to the next. On Isshin→Wolf the pelvis swings from 17° to −76° and back to 76° between two stands, so that move pointed more than 90° away from the pelvis's direction on some frames, and the feet stood 6.7 cm off the hip width there, worse than the 4.2 cm without any move. `blender_full_body.py` now checks `fullbody.held()` with such a swing.
- A foot stands where it moves slower than a quarter of its leg length per second (`STAND`): for a leg of 85 cm, 7 mm per frame at 30 fps. A stepping foot moves faster than a leg length per second; the slow drift of a planted foot stays below the limit.
- Where the target takes the source's root motion, standing and the pelvis's direction are measured where the game puts them: with the source root's move since its first frame, scaled by travel, and its turn about the vertical. In the armature alone, a foot that stands while the root carries the body moves backward on every frame and would never count as standing. Bridge rigs are not parented to their root-motion object; the game puts a bone at the root's location plus the root's turn applied to the rig's pose, and the trials measure the same way.
- The feet are not locked. A source foot that drifts while it stands drifts in the target too, scaled: on Isshin→Wolf the full body's feet moved 2.98 cm in all on the source's standing frames, which is the source's 3.92 cm scaled, against 3.00 cm bone by bone. Locking would fight the source's own small adjustments; the control rig's foot fix does it by hand where it is wanted.
- The status says how far each foot was set out or in, for example "The source's footprint is set to c0000's own hip width: left foot 2.1 cm out, right foot 2.1 cm out."
- With the move, the legs point closer to the source's on both FromSoftware pairs, up to 10.0° away on Owl→Wolf instead of 16.1° and 1.8° instead of 6.4° on Isshin→Wolf, and Wolf no longer lowers his pelvis on the Owl's clip (2.0 cm before).

## Weapon grips and parts with the full-body bake

The bone-by-bone bake had grip and weapon orientation problems that were found only later, so the full-body bake was checked for them. A scratch script outside the repository baked both methods with both **Weapon grip** settings, read-only, and measured each weapon's long axis and flat side, taken from its meshes at rest, against the source's, in the palm and in the world.

- The full-body bake changes only the body. Weapons go into the hand by the same passes after it (weapon turns, grips, Second hand, body clearance, arm distance), and those take the weapon from the final hand. On Isshin→Wolf with the default settings, the katana sits in Wolf's hand within 0.0004 mm and 0.0002° of the bone-by-bone bake, the fingers within 0.0012°, and the parts on its sockets (WP_A_0300 on socket 20, WP_A_0300_1 on socket 147) within 3e-7 of their bones. The hands are up to 3.4 cm and 6.1° apart between the methods, because the bodies under them differ. Second hand holds the grip on frames 25–78 in all four bakes, 0.04 to 0.09 cm off.
- In the world, the blade points up to 18.0° away from the source's with the own grip and 17.4° with the source's grip, in both methods. That comes from **Keep arms close to the body**, not the grip: it turns the whole arm at the shoulder, hand and weapon with it, by up to 22° here. With it and **Keep hands out of the body** off, both methods give the same bake within 0.0002 cm, and the blade points within 0.00° (flat side 0.08°) with the own grip and within 5.75° (9.39°) with the source's, as recorded for 0.11.0, because Wolf's palm differs from Isshin's.
- In the palm, the own grip differs from the source's by 46.5° by design: the weapon sits as in Wolf's own clip, and the wrist turns so that the blade points like the source's.
- Owl→Wolf: the review scene has no weapon mesh on Wolf's R_Weapon, so only the bones were compared. The weapon bone sits in the hand within 0.0005 mm and 0.0001° of the bone-by-bone bake.
- `blender_weapon_grips.py`, `blender_weapon_hands.py` and `blender_weapon_clearance.py` take `-- BODY` and pass with the full-body bake as with the bone-by-bone one.
- Follow-up, for both methods and not changed here, since the bone-by-bone bake stays as it is: **Keep arms close to the body** could turn the wrist back so that a held weapon keeps the source's direction in the world. Whether the weapon should follow the turned arm or the source is a design choice for the user.

## What a rig needs

- Hips and two legs with knees. Without them the bake stops with, for example, "The full-body bake finds no body in the target: it needs hips and two legs with knees. Bake bone by bone instead."
- A spine, and arms with hands for the hand contact. Clavicles, twist bones in series and fingers are optional; spines of two, three and four bones were tested. Names are optional: a target with scrambled bone names bakes the same pose as the named one.
- Matched rest stances (**Rest stance** > *Match source stance*) align the arms, legs and fingers to the source's rest pose, as in the bone-by-bone bake. The spine and neck keep the target's own stance.

## Where it beats the bone-by-bone bake

Measured on owned synthetic rigs by `blender_full_body.py` (numbers in VERIFICATION.md):

- Planted feet slide 29.0 and 29.2 cm over the clip bone by bone on the two targets and 0.00 cm with the full body, and float or sink by up to 1.85 cm against 0.01 cm.
- Hands that the source brings together miss the scaled contact by up to 5.2 cm bone by bone and by 0.00 cm with the full body.
- The spine's chord points within 0.00° of the source's; bone by bone it is 2.35° off on a two-bone spine.
- It needs no bone list for the body, and it spreads chains over other bone counts. The bone-by-bone bake maps such chains end to end, so the bend of the source's middle spine bone is missing from the target's spine (the 2.35° above).

## Where it loses

- Limb directions. To keep the contacts, the legs point up to 12.9° away from the source's on the synthetic rigs, 10.0° on Owl→Wolf and 1.8° on Isshin→Wolf; arms up to 3.2° at a contact. Bone by bone, they point exactly as the source's.
- The source's stride. Forward and back, the feet follow the source's footprint scaled by hip height; only the width is the target's own. A target whose legs are short for its hip height lowers its pelvis to reach a long lunge.
- The source's spine curve. Where the bone counts differ, each target segment lies along a chord of the source's spine, so a bend within that span is lost: a two-bone spine cannot show a three-bone S-curve.
- Wrist twist. A twist bone in series takes its share of the turn between forearm and hand, which the bone-by-bone bake does not do. But a source's twist helpers beside the limb are no part of the chains: they reach the target only through the bone list or automatic matching, and a target without twist bones takes the source forearm's own turn.
- Animated bone offsets. The swing that lays a segment along the source chain turns an animated translation of the next source joint into a rotation: on Owl→Wolf, the Owl's head offset tilts Wolf's neck by up to 4.1°.
- Not handled: toes and the ground (the foot keeps its rotation and the toes follow it), a hand reaching for anything but the other hand, and the weapon-hiding eyes of the bone list when there is no list.

## Decisions made here

- Bone by bone stays the default; the full-body bake is labelled experimental in the menu, the panel, the status and the report.
- The spine and neck keep the target's own stance. Aligning them to the source's rest pose hunched Wolf by about 49° at the neck on Owl→Wolf, because the Owl's rest neck leans 51° forward. The bone-by-bone bake aligns only arms, legs and fingers, for the same reason.
- At a hand contact, a hand that holds a weapon stays and the free hand moves. Moving both moved the katana in Wolf's hand by up to 3.7 cm on Isshin→Wolf, and Second hand then moved the free hand onto the grip anyway.
- A foot that the contact pass moved by less than 0.5 mm gets no note; neither does a hip-width move under 0.5 mm.
- The hip width is taken at the hip joints, the heads of the thighs, measured from the hips. That is where each leg starts, so keeping the source's offset from it, scaled, keeps each leg's sideways lean like the source's, as bone by bone.
- The sideways move holds still while a foot stands and turns with the pelvis while it steps; the feet are not locked. `STAND` is a quarter leg length per second. Reasons in "The target's own hip width" above.
- Weapons: nothing changed. The turn that **Keep arms close to the body** gives a held weapon stays as it is in both methods, because the bone-by-bone bake stays unchanged; it is a follow-up.

Read-only, in background Blender; the scenes were not saved, and the renders were kept outside the repository. ## Arms close to the body (retarget add-on)

- User asked whether biped retargeting in the Unreal sense could help where animations look "too thick": "For example an animation for Corrupted Monk to Wolf. - body_geometry(): each side gains limb = [(bone, local points, post)], the vertices whose strongest bone lies in the forearm's subtree, without weapon parts, carried meshes and placed bones, thinned at 2 × cell. limb_frames() yields per frame the spine's turn from rest, the skinned torso as a BVH tree and the limb points per arm in armature space. nearest_gap(tree, points, limit) gives (distance, point, surface point), unsigned.

A second limb_frames() pass over the frames with a turn bisects (8 steps) any turn whose limb points would come nearer than 0.02 arm. The turned bones are subtree(upper) − placed ∪ held, about the shoulder joint.

- User asked for something that makes imported rigs nicer to animate (they only have point-like bones). - blender_addon/sekiro_retarget/control_rig.py works in the same armature. Each game bone gets COPY_TRANSFORMS "Control rig FK" from MCH-FK-<bone>, a copy of the game edit bone parented to FK-<bone>; IK-driven bones get a second COPY_TRANSFORMS "Control rig IK" from MCH-IKS-<bone> on the IK chain, whose influence is an AVERAGE driver on pose.bones["IK-<end>"]["ik_fk"]. Game rest matrices never change (mesh binding, weapon sockets, the importer's reference pose and the exporter keep working). New bones have use_deform off and carry 'sekiro_control' (kind) and 'sekiro_control_for' (JSON list of driven game bones, read by the exporter). - Sockets get a static pose inv(socket rest) @ game rest (_rest_on). Blender derives rest matrices through the parent (armature_finalize_restpose), and vec_roll_to_mat3 snaps a bone within 2.5e-4 rad of its parent's -Y axis onto it, so a copy under another parent can rest about 0.015° off (Sekiro face bones under face_root).
- FK controls: parent CTRL-Root for skeleton roots, the game parent itself when that is IK-driven and the bone is not (so it follows the blend), else FK-<parent>. Y points at the next joint by anatomy (centre chain, clavicle-arm, leg-toe, finger segments, hand to the middle finger base), else the heaviest child, else continues the parent; the head continues the neck; toes are flattened forward. Z hints: torso forward, clavicle and arms -forward, thigh and calf forward, foot, toe and fingers up. Size = hip height above the feet, else half the bounding diagonal. A bone whose sampled basis scale differs per axis by more than 1e-4 gets a control copied from the game bone (same axes): a turned control would need shear.
- IK per limb: MCH-IK-upper (parent FK-<upper's parent>, COPY_LOCATION from FK-upper), MCH-IK-lower connected with the IK constraint (target IK-<end>, pole Pole-<lower>, pole_angle -90°, chain 2, use_tail, no stretch), lock_ik_y/z, X limit [min(0, -bend), max(0, π - bend)], static pre-bend of max(0, 15° - bend) about X; MCH-IK-end connected with COPY_ROTATION from the IK control, which shares its rest. Hinge: the rest plane normal if the rest bend exceeds 1°, signed toward the measured or natural bend; else the axis the clip bends around, measured in the upper bone's frame; else u × natural (forward for arms, -forward for legs). Chain X = hinge; the elbow side is -Z.
